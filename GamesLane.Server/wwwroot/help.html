Help

Main screen
  • Private games are for family and friends: Create a game, then send the others the link or the game code. Got a code? Enter it to join.
  • Public games are for playing with other people on GamesLane: Create a game, or Join a game to be seated in a game waiting for players.
  • Your icon and name, at the top, open your profile; the flag changes the language.
More details
  • You start as a guest. Log in or create an account in your profile to keep your profile, game order and game options on any device.
  • In your profile you can change your name and icon, turn the chat and sounds on or off, and choose how the cards look.
  • Drag a game by its ⋮⋮ handle to change the order of the games.
  • If you have a game in progress, the main screen offers to Return to it.
  • A good setup: a shared screen (a TV or laptop) watches the game and shows the table, while each player plays on their own phone.
Getting a game started
  • Tap Sit here to take a seat; tap your seat to leave it. Anyone without a seat watches.
  • Once enough players are seated, any player presses Start.
  • The Game options button shows the rules chosen for the game; tap it to change them.
More details
  • Invite others with the link or the game code (🔑, tap it to copy it). The ☰ menu also has the link, and lets you leave your seat or end the game for everyone.
  • 💬 opens the chat, for everyone in the session.
  • In a public game, only its creator changes the options and starts the game; it starts by itself once all the seats are taken.
  • If you lose your connection, your seat is kept: during a game you can come back at any time. If a player is missing when the game needs them, the others can wait, skip their turn (in some games), or end the game.
  • Saved users can save the options as their preferences for the game.
Tic-tac-toe

How to play

  • 2 players, X and O, take turns placing their mark. Three in a row (across, down or diagonally) wins the round.
More details
  • The board has 9 squares. The first seat plays X and the second O. On your turn, place your mark in any empty square.
  • The first player with three marks in a row, across, down or diagonally, wins the round. If all 9 squares are filled without a row of three, the round is a draw.
  • The game goes on round after round, counting each player's wins and the draws. X starts the odd rounds (1, 3, 5…) and O the even ones.
  • The game never ends by itself: leave your seat or the game when you are done.

On this screen

  • Tap an empty square on your turn. The bar at the top turns gold when it is your turn, and shows the score.
  • When a round is over, press Next round.
Backgammon

How to play

  • 2 players race their 15 checkers around the board to their home board, then bear them off. The first to bear off all their checkers wins the game.
  • A match is played to a number of points (5 by default).
More details
  • The board: 24 points, and 15 checkers for each player. The first seat plays white and the second black. The players move in opposite directions, towards their own home board (their last 6 points).
  • The first roll: each player rolls one die. The higher die starts and plays both dice as the first roll. On a tie, both roll again.
  • A turn: roll two dice and move by the numbers rolled: one checker for each die, or one checker for both. A double is played four times (e.g. 4-4 moves 4 four times).
  • Where a checker can land: on an empty point, on your own checkers, or on a single opposing checker, which is hit and put on the bar. A point with two or more opposing checkers is blocked.
  • The bar: a hit checker must enter again in the other player's home board before any other of its player's checkers can move.
  • Using the dice: you must use as many dice as you can (all four with a double). If only one die can be used, it must be the larger one when possible. If nothing can move, the turn passes.
  • Bearing off: once all your checkers are in your home board, move them off the board. A higher number than needed may bear off a checker only when none of your checkers is on a higher point.
  • Winning a game: the first to bear off all 15 checkers wins 1 point; 2 for a gammon (the loser bore off no checker); 3 for a backgammon (the loser also still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board). The points are multiplied by the doubling cube.
  • The doubling cube: before rolling, a player may double the stakes. The other player takes (the game is worth twice as much, and only they may double next) or drops (and loses the game at the cube's current value).
  • Crawford rule: nobody may double in the game right after a player first comes within one point of winning the match.
  • Options: Match length 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9 points (5 by default), and the Doubling cube (on by default; never in a 1-point match).

On this screen

  • Tap the board or 🎲 Roll to roll. Tap a checker, then one of its highlighted points, and press Done.
  • Undo takes back a move; Last move shows the other player's last turn.
More details
  • Double, Take and Drop are in the bar at the top when they can be used; you can also tap the cube when it is outlined to double.
  • A checker that can only go to one place moves at once. A checker can also move by both dice at once, when every way of getting there gives the same result. If there is only one way to play the dice, the game plays it for you.
  • The bar at the top shows the match length, the scores and the pip counts. You see the board from your own side; spectators see it from white's side.
  • Between games, press Next game; when the match is over, Play again starts a new one.
The Gang

How to play

  • 3–8 players, playing together. Each heist, everyone gets 2 secret cards, and up to 5 shared cards are dealt.
  • In 4 stages, each player takes a numbered chip: 1 for the weakest poker hand, up to the number of players for the strongest. You cannot tell anyone your cards; the chips are your only way to talk.
  • The hands are revealed from the lowest red chip up. In order, the vault opens; out of order, the alarm goes off. 3 vaults win, 3 alarms lose.
More details
  • The cards: each player gets 2 pocket cards that only they see. The 5 shared cards come out over the stages: none in the first stage, then 3, 4 and 5. Your hand is the best poker hand of 5 cards from your 2 cards and the shared ones.
  • Hand rankings, from weakest: high card, pair, two pair, three of a kind, straight, flush, full house, four of a kind, straight flush. Within the same ranking, the higher cards win (a pair of 9s beats a pair of 5s). Equal hands may be in either order.
  • The stages: there are four, with white, yellow, orange and red chips. In each, every player takes one chip, numbered from 1 to the number of players, from the middle or from another player. The stage ends when every player holds a chip and everyone agrees.
  • No talking about cards: nobody may say or show anything about their cards. The chips are the only way to tell the others how strong your hand is.
  • The reveal: after the red stage, the hands are shown from the lowest red chip to the highest. If every hand is at least as strong as the one before it, the vault opens; otherwise the alarm goes off.
  • Winning: the gang wins the game with 3 opened vaults and loses it with 3 alarms.
  • Jokers (option, off by default): the deck has two jokers. A player dealt a joker gets two cards instead and keeps one of them. A joker among the shared cards is replaced by two cards, and each hand may use one of the two.
  • Advanced mode (the Mode option): adds challenge cards, which make the heists harder, and specialist cards, which help. The first heist starts with 0 to 3 challenge cards (1 by default); one more challenge card is added after each opened vault, and one specialist card after each alarm. The cards stay for the rest of the game. Players can turn cards off, and can avoid cards that cancel each other.
  • Specialist cards that start "Decide which of you…" are optional: the group may use each of them at most once per heist, at any time during the stages. A group decision takes effect once every player has agreed with 👍.
  • My hand's ranking (option, Hidden by default): when Shown, each player sees the ranking of their own hand so far, next to their cards.

On this screen

  • Tap a chip in the middle or another player's chip to take it; tap your own to put it back. Press Confirm when everyone has a chip.
  • Hide table leaves only the shared cards and your hand, e.g. when a TV shows the table.
More details
  • Any chip change clears everyone's confirmation. A user with several seats confirms once for all of them.
  • 🔒 marks a locked chip, which cannot be taken or put back. A short sound plays when another player takes your chip.
  • Tap a challenge or specialist card to read what it does; tap it again to close it. Cards that have no effect yet are faded. What the cards did is shown in the chip table, under each player's name.
Rich man, poor man

How to play

  • 4–12 players. Get rid of all your cards. Only numbers count: 2 is the lowest and A the highest; suits do not matter.
  • The round's starter throws any number of cards of one number. Each player in turn throws as many cards of a higher number, or passes. When everyone else has passed, the last thrower wins the round and starts the next. Aces win the round at once.
  • The order in which players empty their hands sets the places for the next game: the first is the rich man, the last the poor man.
More details
  • The cards: one set of 2 to A for each player, so everyone gets 13 cards. Suits do not matter. From lowest to highest: 2, 3 … 10, J, Q, K, A.
  • A round: the starter throws any number of cards of one number (e.g. one 5, or three 9s). That number of cards stays the same for the whole round. In turn, each player throws as many cards of a strictly higher number, or passes. A player who passed may throw again when their turn comes back.
  • Winning a round: when the play comes back to the last player who threw, everyone else having passed, that player wins the round and starts the next one, with any number of cards. Aces win the round at once.
  • Emptying your hand: you take the next place in the ranking and stop playing. If the round's winner has no cards left, the next player with cards starts. The game ends when only one player has cards left: they take the last place.
  • Who starts: in the first game, the player with the most 2s (on a tie, the most 3s, and so on); in every later game, the poorest player.
  • Order of play: from the poor towards the rich, then from the richest player straight back to the poorest.
  • Between games: the players are seated by the last game's ranking: the winner is #1, the rich man, and the last is the poor man. The cards are dealt again, then #1 exchanges cards with the last player, #2 with the second-to-last, and so on.
  • How many cards are exchanged (the richest pair first): 4 players: 2, 1. 5 players: 2, 1. 6 players: 3, 2, 1. 7 players: 3, 2, 1. 8 to 10 players: 4, 3, 2, 1. 11 and 12 players: 4, 3, 2, 1, 1. A player in the middle without a pair does not exchange.
  • The exchange: the poorer player of each pair gives their highest cards; the richer player gives any cards they choose. The cards are swapped once every pair has given, and nobody sees what they will get before giving.
  • Fewer than 4 players: there is no exchange, so the next game starts again from the lobby.
  • First game (option): No exchange (the default), or Exchange by current standings: the lobby's seats give the places (seat 1 is the rich man), the pairs exchange before the first game, and the poorest player starts.

On this screen

  • Tap cards of one number, then Select to throw them, or Pass. Tapping a card picks as many as are needed.
  • The bar at the top says what to do.
More details
  • When you start a round, tapping a card picks all your cards of that number; tap a picked card to throw fewer.
  • In the exchange, the poorer player's highest cards are picked for them: press Give these cards. The richer player taps the cards to give and presses Give.
  • The players' table shows the places, each player's cards in hand and their last call. Hide table and Show table hide and show it.
  • A player who is away can be skipped with Skip player. Observers can turn on Read throws aloud.
Wazoo

How to play

  • 1–8 players, 13 rounds. On your turn, roll five dice up to three times, keeping the dice you like between rolls.
  • Then score the dice in one open box of your scorecard. Every box is filled once; the highest total wins.
More details
  • A turn: roll the five dice. You may roll again up to two more times, keeping any dice you like between rolls and rolling the others. Then you must score the dice in one open box, which ends your turn.
  • The upper boxes: Ones to Sixes each score the total of the dice of that number (e.g. three 4s in Fours score 12). If these six boxes add up to 63 or more, you get a 35-point bonus.
  • The lower boxes: Three of a kind and Four of a kind score the total of all five dice. Full house (three of one number and two of another) scores 25, Small straight (four numbers in a row) 30, Large straight (five in a row) 40, and Wazoo (five of a kind) 50. Chance scores the total of all five dice, with no pattern needed.
  • Scoring 0: you may score in a box whose pattern you do not have; it then scores 0. Each box can be used only once.
  • Extra Wazoos: each Wazoo rolled after the Wazoo box already holds 50 gives a 100-point bonus.
  • Joker rule: a Wazoo rolled once the Wazoo box is filled (with 50 or 0) must go in the upper box of its number if that box is open; otherwise in any open lower box, where Full house and the straights score in full; otherwise in any open upper box, for 0.
  • The end: after 13 rounds every box is filled, and the highest total wins (on a tie, all of them win).

On this screen

  • Press 🎲 Roll. Tap dice to keep them (tap again to let go). Tap an open box to score there; it shows what the dice would score.
More details
  • A kept die is lifted and marked Kept. The bar at the top says how many rolls are left, the round, and each player's total.
  • The page asks before scoring 0 in a box. Play again starts a new game once this one is over.
Final Card

How to play

  • 2–10 players. Be the first to play all your cards. Play a card matching the top of the pile by color or by number or symbol, or a Change Color; otherwise draw.
  • Action cards: Stop skips the next player, Change Direction reverses play, +2 and Change Color +4 make the next player draw.
  • With one card left, press Final Card!, or you can be caught and draw penalty cards.
More details
  • Style (option): US Style (the default) plays the UNO rules; Israeli Style plays the Super TAKI rules (below).
  • The cards (US Style): 108 cards. In each color (red, yellow, green, blue): one 0, two of each number 1 to 9, two Stop, two Change Direction and two +2. Also four Change Color and four Change Color +4, which have no color. Each player gets 7 cards, and one card is turned up to start the open pile.
  • A turn: play a card that matches the top card by color, or by number or symbol, or a card without a color; or draw. A Change Color lets you choose the color to play next. You may draw even when you could play.
  • Drawing: draw one card. If you can play it, you may play it or keep it; otherwise the turn passes. When the closed pile runs out, the open pile (except its top card) is shuffled into a new one.
  • Action cards: Stop: the next player misses their turn. Change Direction: play goes the other way (with 2 players, it works like a Stop). +2 and Change Color +4: the next player draws 2 or 4 cards and misses their turn.
  • Penalty cards (from a +2, a Change Color +4, a challenge, or being caught): you must draw them all before you do anything else, and the turn does not pass until you have.
  • Final Card!: press it while you hold your last card, or before playing your second-to-last card, to be protected. It also catches every other player who holds one card and is not protected: they draw 2 penalty cards (4 in Israeli Style). Drawing any card ends your protection.
  • Game end (option): One game: the first to play all their cards wins. Score target (the default; 100, 200, 300 or 500 points, 500 by default): the winner of each hand scores the cards left in the other hands (a number its value, an action card 20, a card without a color 50), and the first to reach the target wins. Pyramid: a player who plays all their cards is dealt one card fewer than last time (7, then 6 … 1), and the first to play their last single card wins.
  • Stacking (option): No stacking (the default), or allowing a +2 on a +2, a +4 on a +4, and a +4 on a +2. A stacked card passes all the cards to draw on to the next player, unless they stack again.
  • Drawing (option): Draw one card (the default), or Draw until you can play.
  • Change Color +4 (option): Always allowed; Only without the color (only when you hold no card of the color to play); or Can be challenged (the default): the next player may challenge instead of drawing. If the player of the +4 held a card of the color to play, they draw the 4 cards instead; if not, the challenger draws 6 cards and misses their turn.
  • Israeli Style cards: 116 cards. In each color: two of each number 1 and 3 to 9, two Stop, two Change Direction, two +2, two Plus and two TAKI. Also four Change Color, and two each of SuperTAKI, Crown, +3 and +3 Breaker. Each player gets 8 cards. Only the Game end option applies: One game or Pyramid.
  • Israeli Style rules: a card you draw cannot be played until your next turn. With 2 players, Change Direction only changes the direction. A +2 can always be answered with a +2; the player who cannot answer draws them all.
  • Plus: play another card; if you cannot, draw. A Plus cannot be your last card.
  • TAKI: play as many cards of its color as you like, one by one, then close the TAKI. Only the last action card in it does its action. A SuperTAKI is a TAKI in the color to play.
  • Crown: can be played on any card, and cancels the +2 cards or the +3 waiting to be drawn; then play any card you like, or draw.
  • +3: every other player must draw 3 cards. The next player can cancel it with a Crown. Any other player can answer it with a +3 Breaker, even out of turn: then the player of the +3 draws the 3 cards. A +3 Breaker played on any other card makes its own player draw 3.

On this screen

  • Tap a card to play it (the cards you cannot play are greyed). Tap the draw pile or Draw a card to draw.
  • Final Card! is always under your cards: it protects you, and catches anyone else who forgot.
More details
  • Under your cards, when they can be used: Draw a card (or Draw N cards), Keep the card after drawing, Close TAKI during your TAKI, and Challenge! against a Change Color +4.
  • The bar at the top shows the color to play and the direction of play (↻ or ↺).
  • The players' table shows 🛡 for a protected player, each player's cards in hand and, with a score target, their points. Hide table and Show table hide and show it.
Yaniv

How to play

  • 2–4 players, 5 cards each. Lower your hand score: One point for Ace, zero for Joker, face value points for 2–10, 10 points for J, Q, K.
  • On your turn, discard one card, a series of the same value, or a straight of 3 or more in one suit; then take a card from the closed pile or the top of the open pile.
  • With 7 points or less, call Yaniv! If nobody is lower, you win the round; otherwise you are Assaf and get 30 extra points.
More details
  • The cards: a 52-card deck and 2 jokers. Each round, every player gets 5 cards; the rest is the closed pile, and the open pile starts empty. The first seat starts round 1, and the winner of each round starts the next.
  • Hand score: the total of your cards' points (e.g. A, 7, 10, K is 1 + 7 + 10 + 10 = 28).
  • Discarding: one card; a series of 2 to 4 cards of the same value (e.g. two 7s); or a straight of 3 or more cards in a row of one suit (e.g. 5♣ 6♣ 7♣). The ace is low only: A-2-3 is a straight, Q-K-A is not.
  • Jokers: a joker can stand for any card in a series or a straight (e.g. 9♥ joker J♥).
  • Taking a card: after choosing what to discard, take the top card of the closed pile or of the open pile. Your discarded cards then go on the open pile.
  • Calling Yaniv: with a hand score of 7 or less, call Yaniv on your turn instead of discarding. Every other player then plays one last turn (they cannot call Yaniv), and all the hands are shown.
  • Who wins the round: if no hand is lower than the caller's (a tie goes to the caller), the caller wins. Otherwise the caller is Assaf and gets 30 points on top of their hand score, and the lowest hand wins. The round's winner gets no points; every other player gets their hand score.
  • Game end (option): One round: its winner wins the game. Score target (100, 150, 200 or 300 points; 200 by default): a player whose points go above the target is out, and the last player left wins.
  • Bonus (option, with a score target; on by default): landing exactly on half the target or on the target takes 50 points off (e.g. with 200, 100 becomes 50 and 200 becomes 150).

On this screen

  • Tap the cards to discard, then tap the pile to take a card from. Yaniv! appears next to your hand score when you can call it.
More details
  • The cards you choose wait next to the open pile until you take a card; they are outlined in red when they cannot be discarded together.
  • After a round, everyone's cards and points are shown. A player who is away can be skipped with Skip player. Hide table and Show table hide and show the players' table.
Hearts

How to play

  • 4 players, 13 cards each. Avoid taking points: each heart is 1 point and the queen of spades 13.
  • Before each hand, pass 3 cards: to the left, then the right, then across; every fourth hand nobody passes.
  • The 2♣ leads the first trick. Follow the suit led if you can; the highest card of that suit wins the trick. Once someone reaches 100 points, the lowest score wins.
More details
  • The cards: a 52-card deck, 13 cards for each player. Play goes clockwise (to the left). Each heart is worth 1 point and the queen of spades (Q♠) 13; the other cards are worth nothing (26 points in all).
  • Passing: before each hand, every player chooses 3 cards to pass: in the first hand to the left, in the second to the right, in the third across, and in the fourth nobody passes; then it starts again. Everyone chooses at the same time, and gets the cards passed to them once all have passed.
  • Tricks: the player with the 2♣ leads it to the first trick. Each player in turn plays one card, following the suit led if they can; otherwise any card. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick (there are no trumps), and the winner leads the next trick.
  • The first trick: nobody may play a heart or the Q♠ on it, unless they hold nothing else.
  • Breaking hearts: hearts cannot be led until a heart has been played in the hand, unless the leader holds only hearts. The Q♠ can be led at any time.
  • Scoring: after the 13 tricks, each player gets the points of the cards they took. Shooting the moon: a player who takes all 13 hearts and the Q♠ gets nothing, and every other player gets 26 points.
  • Claiming: at the start of a trick, the leader whose cards win every remaining trick, whatever the others play, may claim them: the hand ends, and they take all the remaining cards.
  • The end: after a hand in which someone reaches 100 points or more, the game is over and the lowest score wins (on a tie, all the lowest win).

On this screen

  • To pass, tap 3 cards, then Pass 3 cards. To play, tap a card (those you cannot play are greyed).
  • The table at the bottom shows each player's score, and the hearts (♥) and queen of spades (Q♠) they have taken in this hand. Hide table hides it.
More details
  • When you lead and your cards win every remaining trick, Claim all appears under your cards. You can also keep playing. After a claim, everyone's cards are shown face up, so all can check it.
  • Glass breaks when the first heart is played, and a regal doom sounds when the queen of spades is played.
  • Between tricks, the last trick stays on the table with its winner's card outlined. After a hand, Next hand deals the next one.
Dominoes

How to play

  • Block, Draw and All Fives: 2–4 players with a double-six set. On your turn, add a tile whose end matches an open end of the line.
  • Can't play? In Draw and All Fives, draw from the boneyard until you can; in Block, pass. The first to play all their tiles scores the pips left in the other hands; the first to the score target wins.
  • Mexican Train: 2–8 players with a double-twelve set. Build your own train from the engine, the Mexican train, or a train with a marker. Each round you add up the pips left in your hand; the lowest total wins.
More details
  • Style (option): Draw (the default), Block and All Fives, for 2–4 players with a double-six set (28 tiles); Mexican Train, for 2–8 players with a double-twelve set (91 tiles). Players play in seat order, round after round.
  • The deal (Draw, Block, All Fives): 7 tiles each (in Draw and All Fives, 5 each with 3 or 4 players); the rest is the boneyard.
  • Starting: in round 1, the holder of the highest double starts (the first seat if nobody holds one) and plays any tile; in later rounds, the player who played out the round before, or after a blocked round the player after the last starter.
  • A turn: add a tile whose end matches an open end of the line; the matching ends touch. The line has two open ends, and a double is laid like any tile.
  • Can't play? In Draw and All Fives, draw from the boneyard, one tile at a time, until you can play; once it is empty, pass. In Block, pass.
  • All Fives: after each play, if the open ends add up to a multiple of 5 (5, 10, 15 …), the player's side scores that many; a double at an end counts both halves. Reaching the target this way ends the game at once.
  • The end of a round: the first to play all their tiles (Domino!) scores the pips left in the other sides' hands (in All Fives, to the nearest 5). When nobody can play and nothing can be drawn, the round is blocked: the side with the fewest pips in hand scores the pips of the others (on a tie, nobody scores).
  • Score target (option: 100, 150 or 250 points; 100 by default): after the round in which a side reaches it, the highest score wins (on a tie, all the highest win).
  • Teams (option, with 4 players): Partners makes two teams, seats 1 and 3 against seats 2 and 4, who score together; Every player for themselves is the default.
  • Mexican Train: each round has an engine double in the middle: 12-12 in round 1, 11-11 in round 2, and so on down to 0-0 (Rounds option: 13, the default, or 7, down to 6-6). Each player gets 15 tiles (12 with 5 or 6 players, 10 with 7 or 8). The first seat starts round 1, then each round the next seat.
  • A Mexican Train turn: play one tile on your own train, on the Mexican train (anyone's), or on another player's train that has a marker. Every train starts from the engine. Can't play? Draw one tile; if you still cannot play, pass, and your train gets a marker so the others may play on it (playing on your own train removes it).
  • Doubles in Mexican Train: after playing a double, play again. A double at the end of a train must be covered before any other play.
  • The end in Mexican Train: a round ends when a player plays all their tiles, or nobody can play; each player adds the pips left in their hand to their score. After the last round, the lowest score wins.

On this screen

  • Tap a tile to play it (those you cannot play are greyed). If it fits in more than one place, tap where: Left end, Right end or Play here.
  • Draw a tile and Pass appear under your tiles when you cannot play.
More details
  • The line is read from left to right in every language. In Mexican Train, the engine is shown with a row per train: each player's, then the Mexican train, with Open on a train that has a marker.
  • The status bar shows the round, the target, the tiles left in the boneyard and each score.
  • After a round, each side's tiles and pips are shown, with who played out (Domino!) or had the Fewest pips. Next round deals the next one. A player who is away can be skipped with Skip player.
